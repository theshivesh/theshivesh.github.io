Overview

Thousands of writers post stories on Stck, chapter by chapter, and readers pay to read them. But those stories only lived on a screen. Stck Books lets a writer turn their story into a printed paperback. The writer sets it up in a few minutes and a reader orders it in a few taps. Our print partners, Notion Press and Lulu, then print the book and ship it to the reader anywhere in the world. We designed the whole thing: the setup for writers, a quick path for writers who already have a PDF, buying and tracking for readers, the emails in between, and a hand-picked Stck Books imprint for the best books. Today it runs as the Stck Bookshop. Readers buy paperbacks, hardcovers and e-books in English, Tamil, Telugu and Bengali, and more than 75% of each sale goes to the writer or publisher.

Background

Readers kept asking for a real book

Read the comments on any popular Stck story and you'll see the same question: "When can I buy this as a book?" After forty chapters, readers wanted the story on their shelf. Most of the writers were new, and seeing their name on a printed book meant a lot to them. The problem is that turning a story into a printed book is hard, and most of that work has nothing to do with writing.

Problem

Printing a book was full of jargon, and Stck had never sold anything physical

About 30 fields to fill

Trim size, bleed, spine, ISBN, BISAC and pricing all stood between a story and a book you could order.

Many writers aren't technical

A lot of them write in regional languages. A form full of publishing terms would have stopped them.

Easy to price a book too low

Without help, a writer could set a price below the print cost and lose money on every copy.

No shipping, orders or tracking

Stck had never sold a physical product, but buying a book still had to feel as safe as any online store.

Goal

Make printing a book as easy as posting a chapter

Easy for writers

Publishing

Set up a print-ready book in minutes, always priced above cost, with your earnings shown clearly. No need to learn what "bleed" means.

Safe for readers

Buying

Buy a copy of a story you love in a few taps, and feel sure it will arrive.

Room to grow

Business

Add a new way to earn with no stock to hold, and give writers a clear step up: self-publish first, then get picked for Stck Books.

Approach

Break the long form into small steps

  • We split about 30 fields into five simple steps: Start, Upload, Cover, Pricing and Review. A progress bar stays at the top, so the writer only deals with one small thing at a time.
  • There are two ways in. Writers whose story is already on Stck get a guided setup. Writers who already have a finished PDF get a shorter path.
  • Pricing can't go wrong. The screen always shows the print cost, the writer adds their price on top, and their earnings update as they type.
  • We designed the hard moments first: upload errors, details that fail to load, empty order pages and loading screens. These are what real people with real files run into.

The reader's side

Buying the book

Everything the writer set up becomes a store page a reader can buy from in a few taps. Checkout had one job: feel safe. Real couriers, real delivery times, a clear total and a plain note: "typical delivery takes up to 1 week; all sales are final."

The details

Publish a book without learning publishing

The setup hides the hard parts. Add a title. Paste an ISBN if you have one and we fill in the rest. Pick up to three BISAC categories, design or upload a cover, then set a price. The one real choice is how to publish. Self Publish has no Stck branding and goes live right away. Publish with Stck Books carries the Stck Books name after a one to three day review, and looks more official. Readers get a real order timeline (Placed, then Shipped with a tracking link and delivery dates, then Delivered), pre-orders and a proper empty state. We designed the emails too, so nobody is left wondering what's happening.

Reflection

Most of the work was taking things away

The hardest part was everything we hid. A first-time writer can finish setup without ever seeing an ISBN rule or a spine calculation, and that was the point. The other big win was building a store people can trust on a product that had only ever sold digital things. That was a second big project inside the first one. Over about 105 screens, Stck Books taught us that people need a place to start and a goal to work toward. Give them both, and the product grows with them.
